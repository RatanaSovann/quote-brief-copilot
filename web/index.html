Quote Brief Copilot

Turns a messy customer enquiry into a job card, a draft reply and a quote brief. A person approves every message and sets every price. Nothing is ever sent, and the business and enquiries are fictional.

Every quote starts with a back-and-forth

Enquiries arrive in the customer's own words and often miss something the estimator needs. Someone re-types them, works out what's missing and writes back, sometimes twice, before a quote can start.

One enquiry, start to finish

To start a quote, the estimator needs the room size, the finish, the colour and whether it's installed. Kez gave none of them, and asked for a price.

Today

  1. A rep re-types Kez's details into the quote sheet
  2. Works out from memory what the estimator will need
  3. Writes back, careful not to guess a price
  4. Result: Kez answers half; the rep writes again. The quote waits.

2 rounds → 1

With the copilot

  1. The job card is filled in, each detail quoted from the form
  2. The checklist shows the four missing details
  3. A draft asks for exactly those, with no price
  4. Result: the rep reads it and approves. Kez is asked once.

Where the business value is

Hypotheses, each with how it would be measured, mostly from the run log. No savings figures: without real enquiries, any number would be made up.

  • OutcomeWhy it mattersHow it would be measured
  • Faster first replyCustomers asking for quotes often contact more than one business. A quick, clear reply keeps the lead warm.Time to draft and time to decide; % of each draft edited (run log)
  • Ask once, not twiceEvery extra round adds days before a quote and is another chance for the customer to drift away.Rounds per quote, before and after (needs live inbox data)
  • Estimators price, not chaseA complete brief means estimator time goes on quoting, the work that brings in revenue.Flagged fields per brief (run log)
  • No promises it can't keepA price or date guessed in a first reply is hard to walk back. Upset customers get a person, not a template.Screen hits per draft (run log); routing accuracy (evaluation)

One quieter benefit: what "complete" means for each product is written down in one settings file. Today it may live in one experienced person's head; with the copilot, a new rep asks the same questions on day one.

Who it's for

  • Sales repLess typing, and a reply they'd happily send
  • EstimatorA complete brief to price from
  • OwnerControl over what customers are told
  • CustomerA quick reply that asks once

What it doesn't do

It doesn't price, send, or connect to an inbox or CRM. It prepares; a person decides. Those limits are deliberate: they keep the first version safe to trial.

What I'd ask in discovery
  • How many enquiries a week, and through which channels?
  • What does a complete enquiry look like for each product, and who decides?
  • How many back-and-forths does a quote take today, and how long does the first reply take?
  • How often does a lead go quiet before a quote is sent?
  • Where do enquiries live now: shared inbox, CRM, paper?
  • What must a reply never say, besides prices and dates?
